Winger.

Ten decision drills, played from a winger's own eye level. Pick a side, watch a moment of live play, then choose. All of them track the left and right wing separately, so a weaker side shows up on its own.

ShoulderFirst touch

The ball is already on its way to you. Half a second to look up the pitch over your shoulder, then decide where the first touch goes — down the line, 45° in, square inside, or set it back.

PocketMovement

One player per lane, the way Guardiola and Flick coach it. Two blue shirts decide where you go: is your full-back overlapping outside you, and is your 8 in the half-space inside you?

BylineCrossing

You are at the byline with the ball and three runners attacking the box. One glance in, then pick the delivery — near post, cutback, far post, or nobody is on and you keep it.

ThroughFinal pass

Your full-back finds you in the channel. Check inside, take it, and carry it 45° into the half-space with your head up — tap once to glance over your shoulder for the overlap. Then: in behind for the 9, the diagonal for the far winger, into the 10's feet, or down the line for your full-back, if he went.

FinishShoot or slip

You have cut inside and are carrying it across the edge of the box. Is anyone blocking your shot? If not, pick the post the keeper has left you. If someone has stepped out to block you, find the man he left: square to your 9, or the reverse ball to your runner.

Back PostArriving in the box

The ball is on the other wing and you are the far winger. Read the crosser, then your man: hold the edge of the box for the cut-back, arrive on his blind side at the back post, or step in front of him into the middle of the goal.

SnapBreaking the lines

0.7 of a second from the wing or the 10, everyone moving. Find the one ball that breaks their lines — ruptura, a la espalda, entre líneas, apoyo del 9 or cambio de orientación — and tap where it has to go.

Third ManTiming the run

Your 6, centre-back or full-back plays it into your 9 or 10. Man on his back? Go — tap the space you run into the moment it is played in. Free to turn? Stay wide. Early and their defence reads it; late and the second man gets closed down.

One tap: where + when 16 reps Phone sideways
Start
ArriveRead, run, finish

One whole move. Your full-back and your 8 tell you which lane is yours; the pass into your 9 or 10 tells you when to go. Meet the lay-off in stride, carry it at the box with someone sprinting at you — then shoot near or far, square it, or slip the runner.

A tap, then a finish 16 reps Phone sideways
Start
ShapeOff the ball

Juego de posición off the ball. Five seconds of play, often nowhere near you; it freezes as the last pass is struck: tap where you should be, then run there. Five lanes, no more than two of you in a lane — width with the ball, the midfield four without it, the press when you lose it. Learn mode paints the lanes and names the principle.

Tap the map 16 reps Phone sideways
Start
InteriorOff the ball, from the 8

Shape, from the attacking midfielder’s spot: the right interior of a 4-3-3. Come short to help the build-up, get between their lines in your half-space, take the width when your winger comes inside; in the block, jump to their centre-back when we press high, shut the pass back inside when it goes wide. Tap your spot, then run there.

OcclusionRead the passer

Anticipation. Our build-up from your eyes, and the clip cuts before the last pass is struck: tap the team-mate it is going to. The glance, the plant foot, the hips, the backswing give it away; one pass in four is disguised. Three in a row and the cut moves earlier.

Start
NineStriker & false 9

Shape, from the centre-forward. Each rep tells you the role. As the striker, pin their centre-backs on the last line; as the false 9, drop off them into the gap between their lines. Without the ball you are the front of the block: screen their 6, or press their centre-back when we press high. Tap your spot, then run there.
